# How Personal Duets Connect Work Across a Company

## Film purpose

For colleagues and business leaders encountering this design for the first time. After watching, viewers should understand how an employee delegates to their own Duet, how work moves between roles, and where Agent Loop, Message Center and LoopLab participate.

The main story uses a hypothetical regulatory change. Customers need to see the processing status of their applications, and an internal report must export that status. Nora handles market research, Maya owns the product, Alex develops it, and Sam handles review and testing. Bob’s request for a report is a short everyday example at the beginning.

Estimated running time is 8–10 minutes. Each scene unfolds through several actions, introducing names and architecture as the work develops. Use white, graphite and selective red accents. Product controls, messages and narration are in English. The suggested timing is editorial guidance and should be adjusted after recording.

## 01 Each person delegates part of their work

### Viewer takeaway

AI-native work begins with individual delegation. A complete workflow needs outputs the next person can use and a clear condition for completion.

### On-screen progression

About 30 seconds. Start with one employee and their Duet, handing over research, a report and an ongoing follow-up. Pull back to four employees in research, product, development and testing, each with their own Duet. Connect research materials, requirements, code and test results, leaving the endpoint open. Introduce one role or output every five to seven seconds.

### Narration

A company runs through the connected responsibilities of many people. Each person can begin by handing part of their work to an AI, whether that means collecting information, preparing a report or following an ongoing task. In this design, each employee’s personal AI is called Duet. When one Duet’s output becomes something the next person can use directly, those individual delegations start to connect. With a clear starting point, handoffs and acceptance conditions, a business process can keep moving through rules and human judgment. This is how we approach AI-native work.

### Transition

Return to an ordinary message between two people so viewers can see exactly how one delegation begins.

## 02 Bob needs a report

### Viewer takeaway

Employees address their own Duet. Sending as the employee follows an explicit instruction within the current task.

### On-screen progression

About 40 seconds. Bob sends a human direct message asking for this week’s report. The employee selects it and chooses **Ask my Duet**. The quoted request appears in their own Duet, followed by “Prepare this report, then send it to Bob.” Show the data being organized, the report being created and the file becoming ready. Return to the original human conversation, where the employee’s avatar sends the attachment and Bob opens it. Show the instruction before the send action.

### Narration

Bob messages me and asks for a report. I select his message, bring it to my own Duet, and say, prepare this report, then send it to Bob. The quotation carries the original request and the conversation it came from. My Duet organizes the data, completes the report, and uses a sending tool to post the file as me in that original conversation. Sending is part of the task I have just assigned. Bob receives what he needs and continues his work. One small delegation has crossed a handoff between two roles.

### Transition

Reduce the attachment to a completed item and hold on My Duet to introduce the ongoing relationship.

## 03 The same Duet stays with you

### Viewer takeaway

Duet has a continuing identity and responsibility. One front-facing agent handles different tasks and learns the employee’s working practices.

### On-screen progression

About 35 seconds. Keep one **My Duet** entry point on screen as the employee requests a report, asks about a customer and starts industry research. Show three tasks progressing separately in the background, with results returning to the same entry point. Briefly reveal instructions the employee has taught Duet, such as “Include sources in research” and “Explain the impact on current projects.” Earlier messages fold into a summary while recent exchanges remain intact.

### Narration

Duet stays with the same employee over time. You can teach it your responsibilities, preferred sources, working standards and communication style. During a day, you might assign a report, discuss a customer, then ask about another piece of research. You keep addressing the same Duet. It works on the tasks separately and brings their results back. As the conversation grows, older material is condensed into a summary and the latest exchanges remain in their original form. You can continue the immediate discussion and return to ongoing work later.

### Transition

Show Monday on the calendar. An external update arrives, beginning the full business story.

## 04 Message Center makes a change discoverable

### Viewer takeaway

Message Center holds readable information and supports search, subscriptions and retrieval. Information can remain available until someone finds a reason to act.

### On-screen progression

About 40 seconds. A crawler discovers the illustrative regulatory update. Combine its source link, publication date, summary and classification into one information card. Place it in **Message Center** beside customer feedback, industry news and business notifications. Highlight **Search**, **Subscribe** and scheduled retrieval in turn. Then highlight Nora’s research subscription while other information remains available without starting tasks.

### Narration

Imagine that, on Monday, a crawler finds a regulatory update concerning customers’ visibility into application processing. Message Center keeps the source link and publication date, adds a readable summary and classifies the information. Employees can browse, search and subscribe. Their Duets can look for information relevant to their responsibilities or check on a schedule. This brings outside changes into the company’s shared information space. An item can remain here until someone connects it with work they are doing and decides how to respond.

### Transition

Move from the shared information card into Nora’s personal view, explaining why this update matters to her.

## 05 Nora turns information into a research assignment

### Viewer takeaway

Duet uses personal context to identify relevance. The employee decides to invest, and research produces materials the product owner can use.

### On-screen progression

About 45 seconds. Nora’s Duet connects the update to her ongoing work on the customer application experience. It suggests that the existing application page may need a status display. Nora asks it to research the impact, prepare a report and propose requirements. Reveal an impact list, a report with sources and an initial user story. Mark the customer interface and internal report as affected areas, then deliver the material to Maya.

### Narration

Nora handles market research. Her Duet knows she is following the customer application experience, so it recommends this update and explains how it may affect the existing application page. Nora decides to investigate and asks Duet for research, a report and a requirements proposal. The outputs become specific. Which processes are affected? What does the source say? What should customers see, and what should internal staff be able to export? Maya receives these materials and can turn the research into a product requirement.

### Transition

Open the report on Maya’s screen, then expand the view to a human group conversation and its linked agent topic.

## 06 People discuss the work Duets coordinate in a topic

### Viewer takeaway

Human conversations and agent topics support their respective participants. A message shortcut addresses the employee’s own Duet, which can then collaborate asynchronously in the topic.

### On-screen progression

About 50 seconds. Nora asks her Duet to create a human group with Nora, Maya, Alex and Sam, plus a linked topic called “Application status visibility.” Mark **Topic owner · Nora’s Duet**. Nora discusses the requirement in the human group. Maya selects the message and chooses **Ask my Duet**. Draw the connection from Maya to Maya’s Duet, then into the topic. Nora’s Duet posts the research and mentions Maya’s, Alex’s and Sam’s Duets. They add requirements, technical observations and testing questions at different times. Keep a shared file area visible.

### Narration

Nora can ask her Duet to bring the product, development and testing colleagues into a human group. The people discuss scope and priorities there. Maya can take a message that needs work and hand it to her own Duet. Meanwhile, Nora’s Duet creates and manages a linked topic, posts the research and invites the relevant Duets to contribute. Each Duet brings its employee’s responsibilities and uses that employee’s group access to read the shared history and files. People address their own Duet. The Duets coordinate through the topic, leaving work and replies for one another asynchronously.

### Transition

Show responsibilities emerging in the topic. Advance the calendar from Monday to Thursday, when Nora opens the same Duet again.

## 07 Remembering how the work has progressed

### Viewer takeaway

Recent context, Dream and Tracking support immediate conversation, durable knowledge and ongoing work. Git preserves changes and sources, while Duet reasons from the evidence.

### On-screen progression

About 65 seconds. Show Monday’s assignment, several unrelated conversations, then Thursday’s question, “Where are we on application status?” Reveal one memory layer at a time. Recent context contains a summary and intact recent exchanges. Every four hours, Dream incrementally reviews all group conversations visible to the employee and their exchanges with their own Duet. It extracts facts such as Nora’s responsibility and the expectation that research includes sources. Show a Git version with its source. Tracking keeps “Application status response” available as root metadata, then retrieves the chain “Research started → Report delivered → Requirements agreed → Development underway.” Show an updated plan date, from Friday to Monday, with the source and recorded change.

### Narration

On Thursday, Nora asks where the application-status work has reached. Duet uses several forms of memory to recover the work. Recent context holds a summary and the latest exchanges. Every four hours, Dream incrementally reviews all group conversations visible to the employee and their conversations with their own Duet, consolidating lasting information. Git preserves versions and sources. If Monday’s plan conflicts with Thursday’s, Duet can trace the change and update memory from the applicable decision. When Duet recognizes an ongoing matter, it creates a Tracking item. Its first node describes the item and its goal. That metadata stays in everyday context to help locate the work. Detailed progress is retrieved when needed, and new decisions and deliveries add further nodes. Work can continue across many intervening conversations.

### Transition

Expand the “Development underway” node into Alex’s code change, introducing a handoff driven by explicit rules.

## 08 Agent Loop routes an event to the responsible person

### Viewer takeaway

Agent Loop is a separate project that runs without an LLM. Policy files route structured events and outcomes. Duet performs the understanding and execution.

### On-screen progression

About 55 seconds. Alex’s Duet implements customer-facing status visibility from Maya’s agreed story and opens a PR. Show an event with `pull_request.opened`, its PR reference and commit version. Introduce the outer Agent Loop. Reveal a readable policy in stages, “New PR → Sam for review,” “Changes requested → Alex,” and “Approved → Acceptance testing.” Sam’s Duet examines the change and finds the internal report missing the processing status. Show both written feedback and a separate `changes_requested` outcome field. Highlight the matching rule in the outer loop.

### Narration

Maya writes the product requirement as a user story. Alex’s Duet implements it and opens a pull request, or PR, proposing its code changes for review. GitHub emits an event identifying the PR and commit. Agent Loop runs as a separate service and uses a policy file to send the review to Sam. The outer loop follows deterministic rules without calling a model. Sam’s Duet understands the code and checks it against the requirement. It finds that customers can see the status, while the internal report still lacks the field. It returns specific feedback and a structured changes-requested result. Agent Loop reads that outcome and routes the next task back to the author.

### Transition

Follow the change request around the outer loop to Alex, keeping the same PR visible as its version changes.

## 09 The repaired version returns to the workflow

### Viewer takeaway

A result can start the next task. The requirement, PR and commit references keep the work connected across revisions.

### On-screen progression

About 35 seconds. The configured GitHub integration writes the review to the PR. Alex’s Duet receives the issue and its source, adds **Processing status** to the internal report and pushes a new commit. Sam’s Duet reviews the new version and returns `approved`. Agent Loop sends the requirement, version and acceptance conditions to LoopLab. Keep both parts of the user story visible throughout.

### Narration

The rule writes the feedback to the PR and assigns the correction to Alex’s Duet. It receives the missing requirement and the relevant version, adds the report field and pushes another commit. The updated version follows the same review policy. Sam’s Duet checks it and returns an approved result. Agent Loop can then send the change to LoopLab, together with the requirement and the conditions to verify. Each handoff carries context the next participant can use directly.

### Transition

Replace the code view with a real browser and turn the requirements into executable acceptance checks.

## 10 LoopLab verifies the result in a browser

### Viewer takeaway

Test preparation and independent replay are separate stages. Acceptance comes from an actual fixed-version run, and its result can trigger further work.

### On-screen progression

About 55 seconds. Display Sam’s two acceptance conditions. The customer page shows **In review**, and the same application’s exported internal report also contains **In review**. The LoopLab agent investigates the page, saves reusable nodes and composes a flow. Close that investigation view and open an independent browser to replay the fixed version. Highlight the page assertion and exported-file assertion as they pass, retaining steps and screenshots. Show `passed` with a report reference. A failure branch explains that a failed assertion returns evidence to Alex’s Duet, while the main story continues along the passing branch.

### Narration

Sam has supplied two acceptance conditions. The customer should see that the application is in review, and the exported internal record should contain the same status. LoopLab’s agent investigates the real pages and prepares reusable nodes and a test flow. An independent browser then executes a fixed version of that flow, checking the page and export while recording steps and screenshots. Replay follows the defined steps. A passing result returns to the workflow with evidence. If a check fails, Agent Loop can send the failure location and evidence back to Alex’s Duet for another correction and verification.

### Transition

Turn the same run into a readable report and a structured event, sending them to Message Center and Agent Loop respectively.

## 11 Each person can see what their work has achieved

### Viewer takeaway

Shared acceptance evidence supports different personal Tracking records and the decisions still ahead. The same result is useful to several responsibilities.

### On-screen progression

About 40 seconds. Publish the report in Message Center and the linked topic while sending the structured result to Agent Loop. Update Nora’s Tracking with “Research led to a verified feature,” Maya’s with “Requirement accepted, release scheduling next,” Alex’s with “Change verified,” and Sam’s with the test run and evidence. Maya reviews the result with her own Duet and chooses a release date. Show the same report reference in the different records.

### Narration

The acceptance report returns to the shared topic and can be found in Message Center. Nora can see that the change she spotted has become a verified feature. Maya has evidence that the requirement is satisfied and can arrange the release. Alex’s and Sam’s Tracking records capture the correction, verification and supporting evidence. Each person follows the work from their own responsibilities, referring to the same result. When a decision is needed, Duet brings the progress, materials and remaining choices together for its employee.

### Transition

Pull back from the evidence shared across the Tracking records to reveal the full business journey and the outer loop.

## 12 Repeatable handoffs become the next automated workflow

### Viewer takeaway

Personal delegation, shared information, deterministic routing and independent acceptance connect a business process. A company can extend this approach one established workflow at a time.

### On-screen progression

About 40 seconds. Retrace five outputs, the update, research report, user story, PR and acceptance evidence. Label Agent Loop around the outside, then highlight Message Center, the employees’ Duets and LoopLab inside. Fold two recurring handoffs, “Who reviews a new PR?” and “Who receives requested changes?” into a policy file. Keep all four people visible alongside decisions about goals, priorities and release timing.

### Narration

Looking back, Message Center made the change discoverable. Duet understood and carried out the work each person delegated. Agent Loop connected the handoffs through rules, and LoopLab supplied acceptance evidence. A team can first establish how a real piece of work should run, then turn its recurring handoffs into policies defined by the responsible people. Employees continue to decide goals, priorities and commitments. As more outputs become usable inputs for the next role, the company can build its AI-native workflows one process at a time.

### Closing hold

Hold on the complete journey and its final acceptance evidence, giving viewers time to see where the work began, who received each handoff and how it reached completion.
