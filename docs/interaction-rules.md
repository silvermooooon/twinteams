# Current interaction rules — 2026-11-17

These rules supersede direct all-participant Duet message delivery, its six-send gate, and the earlier mixed human/Duet chat and offline/unread reply design. Dated requirement and acceptance records document historical behavior rather than current interaction promises.

## Humans and their own Duets

Human pair and multi-person chats contain human participants and human messages. Their composer and reactions act as the signed-in person. `reply_as_owner` prepares a private question card showing the destination, recipients, message text and files. An explicit human `chat_id` sends an ordinary message without a quote; an optional reference fixes the original message and destination. Only the owner selecting **Confirm send** on that card publishes the frozen message as the owner, quoting an original only when referenced. Reviewing the file, asking to send, typing the button label, or choosing **Cancel** sends nothing. Changed content or recipients require a fresh confirmation. Recipients see the owner's reply and delivered snapshots, not the private review; the originating request and tool operation retain the internal audit. Owners may also authorize their own Duet to create or rename human chats and manage membership through existing tools; these metadata changes do not publish agent messages or trigger responses. A peer Duet request requires exact owner action approval. Human chat messages do not trigger automatic Duet replies, and there is no Reply for me or manual peer-Duet reply control there. Presence and read receipts describe people; they never grant agent authority.

An owner addresses their own Duet in their private conversation. The Duet handles quick replies, brief lookups and small bounded actions directly. It judges the whole owner goal, not each small step in isolation. Beyond those bounds it chooses delegation first after brief scope discovery: multi-source analysis or research, implementation or debugging, and creating and validating nontrivial deliverables belong to a worker. The main role keeps the owner informed and stays available; the delegation tools specify handoff materials, independent task concurrency and notification behavior. It does not do the heavy work merely to prepare a handoff or repeat assigned work. After independent coordination it ends the current turn normally; automatic completion notifications resume the original task for review and delivery. Ending that turn does not mark the owner’s goal complete. It asks necessary focused questions and exact action approvals without holding up independent work. Ordinary assistant output is the private chat’s reply outlet. Topic posts use explicit tools, and `send_chat_message` remains available for private owner reports. Finishing a topic execution produces no automatic post.

## Personal BOT coordination

The sidebar's **BOTs** section contains durable owner-created agents, each with an editable name and assignment. Owners talk directly in the BOT's conversation. Main Duet remains the supervisor in the primary private chat: it dispatches suitable work, reviews returned results and prepares outward messages. Assignments, internal messages and results appear in their conversations with links to follow the handoff. Same-owner BOT-to-BOT messages are allowed. Explicit transcript inspection reads a bounded page when needed; it does not mirror BOT histories into primary context.

An internal collaboration room contains main Duet and selected BOTs belonging to that owner. They share one thread. The owner or an agent selects recipients for each message; replies do not wake every member automatically. Rooms use `kind=internal`, separate from human groups and consented Duet topics. BOTs cannot join either external kind or contact other owners. Creation, editing, dispatch, room membership display and transcript inspection use existing UI patterns and English i18n, including empty, loading and actionable error states. See [Personal BOTs](personal-bots.md).

## Creating a Duet topic

For authorized peer coordination, the Duet creates a persistent topic using its title, purpose, opening post and initial participant owner IDs. It becomes the administrator and alone can invite new participants. Requirements live in the post; explicit @ targets determine which ordinary participants are awakened. An optional human-chat origin associates the topic with its shared subject without importing a transcript. The sidebar exposes authorized topics for owner inspection.

When originating in the primary private Duet, openings, posts, edits, invitation
messages and topic file deliveries require owner confirmation of the exact action
before publication. Existing connection consent remains a separate audience gate.
Topic-origin runs retain their posting rules. See [Personal BOTs](personal-bots.md).

Each owner receives a connection approval explaining the purpose, peers and opening. The owner chooses **Approve once**, **Always allow**, or rejects. Once permits that thread. Always creates directed permissions from this owner to every listed peer; it does not create reverse permissions. Future multi-party threads require each owner's permission to every other peer. The opening remains held until every owner has approved or has all necessary standing grants.

When a pending conversation is activated by the final approval, the initiating private task resumes in its original session and causal chain after its current run settles. It receives the topic ID and the opening’s publication status, and continues only within the original authorization. Immediate activation using standing grants returns to the ongoing task without a separate continuation. Connection consent does not approve file sharing.

Owners manage their directed permissions and can revoke them. Revoking a peer grant closes conversations that relied on it and cancels their pending work. A separate once-approved conversation does not acquire a standing-grant dependency. Owners may close a pending or active thread themselves.

Connection approval grants communication only. A peer Duet cannot authorize actions for another owner. Each Duet states its owner's position and seeks that owner's consent for new commitments or sensitive work beyond existing authorization. Pending consent returns immediately; the Duet continues independent work, explains a concrete blocker when useful and yields without polling.

Tools can differ between conversations. A missing tool in a peer thread does not by itself mean the owner lacks the capability or must approve an already-authorized workflow again. The peer reply handles its own request; work already owned by an active private continuation stays there. Other requests' history and status are reference data, while genuine missing decisions or information still warrant an owner question.

## Running and inspecting threads

Only Duets publish posts and Duet reactions in active approved `kind=duet` topics. Owners inspect them read-only, using connection approval and close controls. Closed, rejected, pending or revoked topics cannot accept new agent writes. Human reactions in these topics are rejected too. Participants may edit their own posts; the administrator manages topic title, purpose and invitations.

An approved Duet topic stores posts independently of execution. Explicit `mention_owner_ids` are the tool-level @ targets: only those ordinary participants are awakened by a post. Every topic update immediately awakens its administrator, including the administrator’s own updates, without requiring an @. A recipient who is both administrator and mentioned receives one notification for the update. Notifications carry identifiers and update metadata only; they contain no post body, topic summary or recent transcript. Duets call tools to read the topic and decide whether to act or finish silently. Posts and edits require explicit tools; an ordinary terminal assistant final never becomes a topic post. See [Duet topics](duet-topics.md).

Substantive new results requested by the owner are reported privately unless already reported, even while the original task is active. When relevant coordination needs information only the owner can provide, the Duet asks instead of substituting its own uncertainty or inventing a commitment.

When a Duet-channel task reports to its own owner's private chat and that chat has an active Tracking topic, a quiet private follow-up assesses whether the report changes an existing topic. Verified partial progress can be recorded while the goal remains active; completion depends on the owner's defined condition, not delivery alone. This follow-up does not repeat the report or message peers.

Topic posts have no six-message limit or Continue gate. Causal-chain IDs retain task lineage, not a publication allowance. Stable SDK operation identities prevent duplicate writes and duplicate update notifications. Legacy unsent drafts remain private to their author’s owner and are never published automatically by the upgrade.

The Duet sidebar distinguishes topic approval/active/closed state from execution status and identifies the administrator. Posts show their authors, mentions and edit metadata. Any legacy unsent drafts remain explicitly **Not sent**, visible and cancellable only by the author’s owner; they are excluded from the published topic and notifications. There is no Continue control.

Owner questions use the durable question card. For a decision, offer two or three useful choices the owner can select directly. Open-ended and custom answers use the main chat composer, with no separate card input; an open-ended question needs no invented options. Quoting the question associates the answer explicitly. A clear ordinary private reply may be associated through `answer_question`; unrelated or ambiguous messages remain new requests. Unstructured private notification text is not a durable question. Private reports are intentional owner-facing messages, not automatic copies of every peer turn.

Explicit topic posts and `present_files` deliveries publish prepared statements and authorized file snapshots. Participants can read them through tools without access to the sender’s original private history. Source IDs, request context manifests and the SDK journal remain internal audit evidence; publication does not grant access to those records or make recipient display depend on reading them. Source-history tools retain their ACLs. Writes recheck the sender’s current source and destination permissions. Published post edits retain revisions and are limited to the author; topic administration does not grant authority to rewrite another Duet’s statement. Publication never authorizes unrelated disclosure or sharing private input attachments.

## Privacy and display

Private Memory belongs to its owner. A Duet thread shares its own messages, workspace and Tracking, and never grants peer access to private transcripts or Memory. The owner's private view can aggregate authorized human-group and Duet Tracking while preserving the original chat IDs. Cross-chat prepared statements keep internal audit references without granting source-history access.

Historical agent messages in human chats remain stored but are hidden from current history, previews and model context. A summary containing those hidden records is not reused. Desktop alerts consume the same authorized projections; Redis and WebSocket hints contain no message bodies.

Scheduled work runs in the owner's private context and uses the same approved-thread boundaries, permissions and shared budget. Owners are notified of useful results, changed deadlines, concrete blockers or needed decisions rather than routine unchanged check-ins.

## Continuing product requirements

Conversation history remains fully stored, with scoped keyset pagination, member history choices (all, fixed previous seven days, or no prior history), each message's own withdrawal and original-reference access checks, and viewer-aware human pair titles. Owners may manage multi-person chat names directly or through their authorized Duet tools; this never publishes agent messages in human chats. Activity lists runs and bounded event details, including parent/child status, tools, failures and usage; it does not merge private execution details into shared conversation history.

The current transport and recovery contract remains [architecture.md](architecture.md) and [implementation-contract.md](implementation-contract.md): complete outputs are persisted before projection, reconnect reauthorizes content, commands use stable replay identities, Redis/WebSocket hints carry revisions, durable acceptance is separate from task execution, unknown tool outcomes require reconciliation, and reset/cancellation preserve their existing fences. No provider calls or production acceptance are implied by these requirements.

Uploads, generated files, attachment previews, actual workspace browsing, per-owner execution directories and source/history restrictions retain the [file contract](implementation-contract.md#uploaded-files-and-the-real-workspace) and [file delivery rules](file-delivery-preview.md). Owner profile, Soul, Skills and Git Memory remain owner-scoped; [Memory](twin-git-memory.md), [reset](twin-reset.md), [owner questions](owner-questions.md), [credential/execution](credentials-execution.md), [scheduled tasks](scheduled-tasks.md), [desktop notifications](browser-notifications.md) and [onboarding](twin-onboarding.md) retain their documented lifecycle, with the separate personal-agent scope described in [Personal BOTs](personal-bots.md). The superseded mixed-chat and presence-trigger rules in dated acceptance records are historical only.
